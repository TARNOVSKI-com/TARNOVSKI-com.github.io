The Zelensky Effect: Strategic Branding Lessons from a Political Victory

2019-03-31

In the captivating journey of Volodymyr Zelensky from a comedian to the President of Ukraine, brands can unearth valuable lessons on perception, strategy, and consistency. The blending of Zelensky's identities - the candidate, the businessman, and his TV persona, Mr. Goloborodko, offers a profound lesson on the power of narrative fusion. This narrative complexity mirrors the intricacies of building a compelling brand story that resonates on multiple levels.

  1. It's easy to mistake fiction for reality

The blending of Zelensky's identities - the candidate, the businessman, and his TV persona, Mr. Goloborodko, offers a profound lesson on the power of narrative fusion. As a spectator, it's complicated to remember three narrative lines and to distinguish them, especially when they all have the same face (in the direct sense) and when you constantly see them all alternating one after another at a fast pace. This narrative complexity mirrors the intricacies of building a compelling brand story that resonates on multiple levels.To craft an indelible brand presence, it's crucial to utilize every narrative channel available. From leveraging the brand through traditional communication to embodying the brand's ethos in the leadership and workforce, each touchpoint is a thread in the tapestry of your brand's story. The goal is to weave these threads into a coherent narrative that stands as an incontrovertible truth in the consumer's mind.

2. Every success is based, above all, on a coherent strategy (and a lot of patience)

Zelensky’s pre-election strategy underlined the significance of strategic foresight and patience. The narrative crafted in the "Servant of the People" TV series wasn't an overnight creation, I'm quite sure, but a meticulously researched project that resonated with the populace's yearning for relatable leadership. In the TV series, viewers received the exact (perhaps utopian) thing they dream of in everyday life — a man like them, 'from the people', as a leader; a simple, but vertiginous man in the most honourable post. People asked for it, and the producers gave it to them. And, most importantly, they planned and executed it three years before the "big event". Very well done! This exemplifies the essence of long-term investment in brand strategy over quick wins.

3. Consistency is everything

Consistency emerged as the cornerstone of Zelensky’s success, maintaining a steadfast message from campaign to presidency. Many believe that the relationship between brand and consumer is important only until the moment of purchase. What happens afterwards is that many brands no longer invest in the company-customer relationship and often ignore the continuity of the brand experience. The problem is that this brand experience is actually created and "grounded", in fact, after the purchase. This consistency is a beacon for brands, illustrating the value of sustaining the brand-consumer relationship well beyond the initial transaction. The post-purchase experience is pivotal in cementing the brand in the consumer's life, thus fostering lasting loyalty.

4. Consistency is really EVERYTHING

In a rather poor country, in which the last two or three generations were educated in communism (aka lack of entrepreneurial spirit and dependence on "social" help), people are unlikely to accept the idea of libertarianism http://bit.ly/ze-lib). But by all "symptoms" this seems to be the most logical and consistent continuation of the 'discourse'. The consistency in advocating for libertarianism, despite societal predispositions, showcases the power of a coherent brand ideology.
To bring up a good example - brands like Barilla have navigated controversies by staying true to their core brand identity, proving that unwavering adherence to your brand’s essence can transcend challenges.

In an era where brands are scrutinized for authenticity and values, Zelensky's journey and other brand stories like McDonald's thriving amidst health trends, underscore the imperative of strategic thinking, narrative coherence, and the unyielding consistency of brand identity.
